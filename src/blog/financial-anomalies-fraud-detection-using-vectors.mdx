title: Financial Anomalies and Fraud Detection using Vectors

* **The Hook:** Walk through a classic fraud rule: `IF amount > $5,000 AND
  country != 'US' THEN flag_fraud()`.
* **The Problem:** Fraudsters know these thresholds. They don't steal $10,000
  all at once; they make odd $45 purchases at weird hours or in strange
  categories that don't trigger scalar rules.
* **The Goal:** What if your database could detect when a transaction simply
  **looks or feels out of place**, without you writing 500 brittle `IF/ELSE`
  statements?
* **The Teaser:** You don't need a dedicated data science platform to do this;
  you can do it right inside MariaDB using geometric distance.

## Thinking in Dimensions

*Goal: Demystify vectors before ever introducing the word "embedding."*

* **1 Dimension (The Number Line):**
  * Start with a single property: **Transaction Amount**.
  * A 1D line from \$0 to \$10,000.
  * \$12 (lunch) is close to \$15 (lunch). Both are far away from \$2,500 (new
    laptop).
* **2 Dimensions (The Flat Grid / Graph):**
  * Add a second property: **Time of Day** (0 to 24 hours).
  * Now we have an $X$ and $Y$ axis.
  * An \$8 transaction at 8:00 AM (coffee) sits right next to another \$9
    transaction at 8:15 AM.
* **3 Dimensions (The Physical Room):**
  * Add a third property: **Distance from Home** (in miles).
  * Now transactions are floating points in a 3D cube.
  * Regular grocery runs cluster together: low price, daytime, low distance.
* **Breaking the Visual Barrier: N-Dimensions:**
  * Humans cannot picture 4D, 50D, or 768D space, but **the math does not
    change**.
  * Each extra detail (merchant category, device signature, memo keywords,
    purchase frequency) is just another axis on the coordinate grid.
* **The Big Reveal (The "Vector"):**
  * Explain that a "vector" is simply this point's address: an array of numbers
    representing its position along all those axes (e.g., `[12.50, 8.5, 1.2,
    0.44, ...]`).

## From Normal Transactions to Vectors

*Goal: Ground the concept in familiar relational database terms.*

* **The Familiar Ground:**
  * Show a standard relational table the reader already has:
    ```text
    transactions (id, user_id, amount, merchant_name, description, created_at)
    ```
* **Introducing "Embeddings" Gently:**
  * Define an embedding model simply: a tool that takes messy, complex data
    (descriptions, memos, behavior) and translates it into that list of
    coordinates.
* **Adding the Vector to MariaDB:**
  * Highlight how natural it is: you don't migrate data; you simply add a
    `VECTOR` column and an index to your existing table.
  * The coordinates live right alongside the user ID, invoice number, and
    timestamp.

## How "Normal" Behavior Clusters

*Goal: Solidify the concept of similarity and clustering before pivoting to
anomalies.*

* **The Daily Routine:**
  * A typical customer's financial life forms a "neighborhood" in
    high-dimensional space:
    * Weekday transit passes.
    * Nearby lunch spots.
    * Recurring subscriptions.
* **Visualization Suggestion for the Post:**
  * *Tip:* Include a conceptual 2D scatter plot (or sketch) showing dozens of
    dots clustered tightly together labeled *"Alice's Normal Habits"*
    (groceries, bills, lunch).
* **Measuring "Closeness":**
  * Explain that MariaDB can calculate the distance between any two points
    (using cosine or Euclidean distance).
  * High similarity = tiny distance.

## Inverting Vector Search for Anomaly Detection

*Goal: Deliver the core insight of the article.*

* **The Search Paradigm We're Used To:**
  * Vector search usually asks: *"Find me the 5 things closest to this point."*
* **The Inversion (The Eureka Step):**
  * What happens when you check the distance to the nearest neighbor, and the
    closest point is **still miles away**?
  * If a new transaction lands in an empty, unpopulated region of Alice's
    coordinate space, it means **this customer has never done anything
    conceptually like this before**.
* **Concrete Financial Scenario:**
  * Alice's regular habits are clustered.
  * A new transaction arrives: \$48 at 3:00 AM for *"Cryptocurrency gift card
    exchange"*.
  * The amount (\$48) is small enough to bypass traditional high-dollar alerts,
    but in vector space, it is stranded completely outside her normal cluster.
  * **Result:** Instant anomaly flag.

## How It Looks in Practice

*Goal: Show the developer that implementation is straightforward.*

* **The Logic in Pseudo-SQL:**
  * When a new transaction arrives, find its distance to Alice's closest past
    transactions:
    ```sql
    SELECT VEC_DISTANCE_COSINE(embedding, :new_transaction_vector) AS distance
    FROM transactions
    WHERE user_id = :user_id
    ORDER BY distance ASC
    LIMIT 1;
    ```
* **The Decision Rule:**
  * If `distance > threshold`, send an SMS verification or pause the transaction
    for manual review.
* **Why MariaDB Shines Here:**
  * Point out that you don't need a separate vector database cluster or complex
    sync pipelines.
  * Because MariaDB handles vectors natively, you can do hybrid checks in a
    single query: filter by `user_id` or date range, calculate distance, and
    trigger the alert in milliseconds within your existing transaction loop.

## Not Just a Gimmick

* **Key Takeaway:** Vector technology isn't just a gimmick for AI chatbots or
  search boxes; it's a way to measure **context and normality**.
* **Closing Thought:** Challenge the reader to look at their current database
  tables: What other data forms "clusters" of normality where outliers would be
  valuable to catch (e.g., error logs, login events, user review patterns)?
